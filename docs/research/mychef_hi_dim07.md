## 2. HAWAII TAX & LEGAL FACTS

### 2.1 General Excise Tax (GET) — verified against Hawaii Dept. of Taxation

- **Structure:** Hawaii has NO sales tax. GET is a gross-receipts tax on the *business* (not the consumer) covering nearly all activity — retail, **services (incl. catering/chef services)**, contracting, rents, commissions. Base rate **4.0%**; wholesale 0.5%; insurance commissions 0.15%. Owed on gross receipts even in loss years; no grocery exemption. [^26^][^27^]
- **County surcharges (2026):** ALL four counties levy **+0.5%** → combined **4.5%** everywhere in Hawaii. Official DOTAX page: Honolulu 4.7120% max pass-on (1/1/2007–12/31/2030); Hawaii County 4.7120% (1/1/2020–12/31/2030); Kauai 4.7120% (1/1/2019–12/31/2030); **Maui 4.7120% (1/1/2024–12/31/2030)** — Maui's surcharge is the commonly-missed update (Ordinance 5551). [^28^][^29^]
- **Visible pass-on:** Businesses MAY (not must) visibly pass GET to customers. Max pass-on rate **4.7120%** in all four counties (4.166% where no surcharge applies). The gross-up exists because passed-on tax is itself taxable gross income (0.045 ÷ 0.955 = 0.047120). Consumer-protection law prohibits visibly collecting MORE than the tax actually owed; rounding 4.166%→4.167% is prohibited. A business cannot claim "no tax charged" if it is subject to GET. [^27^][^28^][^30^]
- **Registration/filing:** GET license via Form BB-1, one-time **$20**, before first revenue; file Form G-45 periodically (20th of following month) + G-49 annual reconciliation; multi-county businesses file Form G-75; rate follows where the service is *performed*, not HQ location. Late filing: 5%/month up to 25% + interest. [^27^][^31^]
- **Practical implication for myCHEF:** quote prices "plus 4.712% GET" (as A Catered Experience does verbatim: "A 20% service fee and 4.712% tax added to total" [^21^]) on every island; track revenue by island of performance.

### 2.2 Service Charge vs. Gratuity — Hawaii law

- **HRS §481B-14 (enacted 2000):** any hotel or restaurant applying a **service charge** for food/beverage services must either **distribute it directly to employees as tip income** or **clearly disclose to the purchaser** that it is used for costs/expenses other than wages and tips. Enacted after deceptive-practice concerns; 2008 lawsuits (e.g., Kahala Hotel wedding reception, 19% service charge) targeted non-disclosure. [^32^]
- **A "service charge" is legally NOT a tip** (state + federal): treated as wages — taxed, counts toward overtime, cannot fund tip credit. If the house keeps any portion, disclosure to guests is mandatory. [^33^]
- **Pending/trending:** SB1256 (2025 session) would have restricted mandatory restaurant gratuities (parties ≥10, ≤18%, advance notice) — BillTrack lists it as **Dead** in committee; shows legislative direction on junk-fee/service-charge transparency. **REQUIRES LEGAL VERIFICATION** of final 2025–2026 session outcomes. [^34^]
- **Tipping custom:** 15–20% customary for private chef service in Hawaii guides [^23^][^24^]; observed actual average tip on Take a Chef Honolulu bookings = **14.72%** of bill. [^5^]
- **myCHEF guidance:** label any service charge conspicuously, state whether it is distributed to staff, and never call a mandatory charge a "gratuity." **REQUIRES LEGAL VERIFICATION** for exact disclosure wording under HRS §481B-14.

### 2.3 Alcohol / Liquor Rules for Private Chefs & Caterers

- **Hawaii is the only U.S. state with exclusively county-level liquor licensing** — four independent County Liquor Commissions (Honolulu, Maui, Hawaii, Kauai); rules and fees differ per island. [^35^]
- **Caterer's Liquor License = Class 13** in all four counties (Kauai hotel license is Class 12). Indicative cost ~**$900–$950/year**; special-event permits ~$45–$90/event. [^36^][^37^]
- **Maui County Class 13 rules (MC-08, Chapter 101, verbatim extracts, rules current to March/June 2026):** caterer license generally granted to an existing on-premise licensee (e.g., Class 2 restaurant) with ≥30% of gross revenue from food; "At least thirty percent of the gross revenues of the catered event shall be food sales"; each off-premise catered function requires prior written application **at least seven working days** before the event, a floor plan, property-owner authorization, and a signed statement that the function is subject to liquor-law inspection; **"Catered functions for which patrons are being assessed a fee is prohibited"** (no ticketed/cash-bar events); licensees must carry **≥$1,000,000 liquor liability insurance**. [^38^]
- **Practical norm observed:** caterers/private chefs typically do NOT serve alcohol — A Catered Experience: "Our staff is not authorized to pour or serve alcoholic beverages" and refers clients to licensed bartending companies [^21^]; restaurants/venues offer BYO with no corkage (e.g., CJ's Maui: "Bring Your Own Beer or Wine – No Corkage Fee!" [^39^]). Airbnb's Hawaii host guidance: "selling alcohol requires a license or a hired caterer, and licenses are typically not available for private residences." [^37^]
- **Whether a private chef may legally *serve* (pour) client-supplied alcohol at a private event in a private home, and whether charging for bartending triggers licensing, is a gray area varying by county commission — REQUIRES LEGAL VERIFICATION with each county Liquor Commission (Honolulu Liquor Commission, Maui Dept. of Liquor Control, etc.).** Hawaii also bans happy-hour pricing; servers must complete alcohol-server education. [^35^]

### 2.4 Food Safety / Catering Permits (Hawaii DOH)

- **HAR Title 11, Chapter 50 (Food Safety Code)** regulates the food industry; "food establishments include: restaurants, **caterers**, lunch wagons, push carts, markets, liquor establishments, … hotel banquet and room service kitchens, … temporary food sales events…" — i.e., caterers are permitted food establishments subject to inspection (placarding system). [^40^]
- **Permits are island-specific:** DOH Food Safety Branch runs separate offices (Oahu, Hilo, Kona, Maui/Wailuku, Kauai/Lihue) with **no statewide reciprocity** — operating on multiple islands requires registration with each district office. [^41^]
- **Fee structure (risk-based, mobile/temporary):** Category 1 (full menu/raw proteins) **$300/yr**, Category 2 **$200/yr**, Category 3 (prepackaged) **$100/yr**; plan review $200 new construction / $150 remodel; official Oahu food-establishment application form includes a "Catering" category. [^41^][^42^][^43^]
- **Certified Food Protection Manager (CFPM)** required for nearly all prepared-food permit classes; food-handler training renewal every 3 years. [^44^]
- **Homemade food exemption (Act 195, rules effective Aug 24, 2025):** HAR 11-50 exemption for non-TCS/shelf-stable home-kitchen foods only (now incl. pickles/ferments pH ≤4.2, online orders, in-state shipping) — **does NOT cover hot meals/private-chef services**. [^44^]
- **Gray area:** a private chef cooking in a *client's home/vacation-rental kitchen* from groceries bought for that client (no off-site prep, no public sales) is treated differently from a caterer with a commissary in many jurisdictions; Airbnb guidance notes "if you prepare food or ingredients before arriving at a guest's home, you likely need a catering license." Exact DOH position on in-home private chef services — **REQUIRES LEGAL VERIFICATION** with DOH Food Safety Branch (808-586-4400). [^37^][^45^]

### 2.5 Labor Law Touchpoints
- **Minimum wage: $16.00/hr from Jan 1, 2026** (Act 114 schedule; $18.00/hr Jan 1, 2028). Tip credit $1.25 (base $14.75) only if wages+tips ≥ **$23/hr** — one of the highest thresholds in the U.S. Overtime after 40 hrs/week. Act 115 (2025): minimum $500 civil penalty for wage violations. [^46^][^47^]

## 4. COST-OF-OPERATIONS CONTEXT

### 4.1 Food Cost Premium vs. Mainland
- Hawaii imports **~85–90% of its food**; grocery price index **131.4** (~31% above national baseline); other 2026 indices put groceries **~50–53% above U.S. average**. Examples: milk $7–9/gal (vs ~$4), eggs $5–8/doz, ground beef $7–10/lb, bread $5–7. [^63^][^64^][^65^]
- SmartAsset 2025: Hawaii grocery prices +5.3% YoY (cereals/bakery +6.5%, produce +6.4%). [^66^]
- Honolulu CPI-U: **Food & Beverages +3.8%** H2 2025 — fastest-rising major category; overall Honolulu inflation hit 5.1% May 2026. [^67^]
- Dining benchmarks: plate lunch $12–18; mid-range sit-down $25–45/pp; fine dining $60–120+/pp; high-end Wailea restaurants $100–150/pp. [^64^][^23^]

### 4.2 Labor Cost Context
- **BLS OEWS: Chefs & Head Cooks (35-1011), May 2025:** Hawaii median **$77,360/yr ($37.19/hr)** vs U.S. median $62,470 — Hawaii is the #2-paying state; 90th percentile $118,000. Kahului-Wailuku (Maui) median **$84,990**; Hawaii/Kauai nonmetro median $80,820; Urban Honolulu median $74,380. May 2023 means: Hawaii $99,520/yr ($47.84/hr) — then the highest-paying state; Kahului-Wailuku-Lahaina mean $104,180 — the highest-paying metro in the nation for chefs. [^68^][^69^]
- Line cooks in Hawaii earn **$44,000+** (top-tier state). [^70^]
- Minimum wage $16/hr (2026) → $18 (2028); unemployment just **2.4%** (March 2026) — extremely tight labor market, upward wage pressure on servers/bartenders. [^46^][^71^]
- Competitor staffing rates reflect this: servers/sous $50–$75/hr billed; $225/server/event; bartenders $50/hr. [^12^][^13^][^14^]

### 4.3 Inter-Island Logistics (pricing architecture relevance)
- Inter-island flights: **~$80–$120 one-way** booked a month+ ahead; walk-up fares ~$150 (2026). [^72^]
- No inter-island vehicle ferry for consumers; equipment moves via Young Brothers barge (~$1,000–$2,500 one-way for a vehicle/truck) — effectively prohibiting casual inter-island service with equipment. [^41^]
- DOH food permits are per-island (no reciprocity) [^41^]; GET multi-county reporting required (Form G-75) with the rate following where the service is performed [^28^][^31^] — all arguing for **per-island chef rosters rather than fly-in coverage** as the default operating model.

## 5. PRICING IMPLICATIONS FOR myCHEF

1. **Anchor per-person pricing at or slightly above the marketplace band:** Take a Chef averages ~$175/pp statewide (Maui/Kauai/Big Island), $106–$210/pp by group size; a premium myCHEF tier at **$150–$250/pp for multi-course dinners** is defensible on Maui/Kauai/Kohala where PPPD visitor spend is $294–$326/day. [^2^][^3^][^4^][^55^]
2. **Publish hourly architecture:** market norm is Chef **$115–$150/hr**, sous/server **$50–$75/hr**, shopping **$55/hr**, **groceries billed separately at cost** — transparency here is a competitive differentiator (many competitors hide behind "call for quote"). [^12^][^13^]
3. **Always quote "+ GET":** 4.5% combined on every island; visible pass-on max **4.7120%**; the rate follows where the service is performed; multi-island operations file Form G-75. Never advertise "tax-free." [^28^][^30^]
4. **Service charge discipline:** if myCHEF adds a service charge (market norm 18–22%; ACE charges 20%), HRS §481B-14 requires it be distributed to staff as tips OR clearly disclosed otherwise — and it cannot be labeled "gratuity." Display the customary 15–20% tip guidance (observed actual: 14.72%). [^21^][^23^][^32^][^5^]
5. **Alcohol = partner model:** do not sell/serve alcohol without a county Class 13 caterer license (which itself generally requires an existing on-premise license, 30% food revenue, per-event approval ≥7 working days ahead, $1M liquor liability insurance, and prohibits charging patrons a fee for drinks). Safer norm: client BYO + referral to licensed bartending companies, exactly as A Catered Experience does. **REQUIRES LEGAL VERIFICATION** before any wine-pairing or bar-service add-on. [^38^][^21^][^37^]
6. **Zone-based travel fees are market-accepted:** Kauai competitor charges $50–$75 flat/day by region + group minimums for remote areas; Big Island caterers charge $100–$500 delivery by zone. myCHEF can publish island zone maps with flat travel fees. [^13^][^20^]
7. **Holiday premiums are standard:** $150/hr holiday chef rate (Kauai), "higher rates for holidays/festive season" (Chef Kristin) — build a published holiday surcharge calendar (Thanksgiving–NYE, Valentine's, Mother's Day). [^13^][^12^]
8. **Deposits:** $2,000 or 50% of event cost (whichever less) is a published Big Island benchmark; food minimums ($2,000–$5,000 by party size) are standard on Oahu. [^20^][^21^]
9. **Target accommodation mix:** ~1 in 4+ visitors stays in condos/rental homes with kitchens; vacation-rental ADRs ($408–$632/night) signal guests already spending $3,000–$4,500/week on lodging — a $175–$250/pp chef dinner is a small marginal spend for that segment. [^52^][^57^]
10. **Weddings/events:** Hawaii avg wedding $53,369 with 74–84 guests; catering benchmarks $60–$75/head (Oahu buffet basis) to $125–$200+/pp (Maui premium) — position myCHEF wedding/intimate-event menus in the $95–$175/pp full-service band with explicit staffing/rental/GET line items. [^60^][^22^][^23^]
11. **Cost pass-through:** food costs ~31–53% above mainland and chef median wages ~24% above national — justifies Hawaii-specific (not mainland-template) pricing and the "groceries at cost" model to avoid margin erosion from CPI food inflation (+3.8%). [^64^][^68^][^67^]
12. **Per-island operations:** per-island DOH permits, per-county liquor rules, multi-county GET reporting, and inter-island logistics costs all favor building island-specific chef teams and island-specific pricing pages (Take a Chef's own per-island price variation — e.g., Kauai 2-person $210 vs Big Island $169 — validates island-differentiated rate cards). [^41^][^38^][^28^][^3^][^4^]
